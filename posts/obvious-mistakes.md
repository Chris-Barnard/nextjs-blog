In his book "The Mental Game of Trading", Jared Tendler ([@JaredTendler](https://x.com/jaredtendler)) writes about the different kinds of mistakes that you can make.
He divides them into three types, *Learning Mistakes*, *Marginal Mistakes*, and *Obvious Mistakes*.
 - Learning Mistakes - Unavoidable weakness in your tactical decision-making.
 - Marginal Mistakes - A blend of weakness in your tactical decision-making with mental or emotional flaws.
 - Obvious Mistakes - Mental or emotional flaws caused your emotions to be too intense.

# Why do we make mistakes that we know are wrong?

Why on earth would I make a mistake that I know to be a mistake seconds after taking the action?
I would argue that we make these mistakes because our thought process has been hijacked by emotional volatility.
Focusing on improving our knowledge of trading or improving our tactics can't help us to improve our performance in moments that we don't have access to this information because the emotional pull is too strong.

So if trying to improve our knowledge isn't the answer, how do we avoid making these obvious mistakes?
If your answer is to develop monk like emotional control, then you're accepting that you'll always be diverting some of your mental energy into trying to control your emotions.
This is a strategy that is draining, and in the long run, makes it impossible to reach your potential as a trader.

# Control isn't the solution.  Instead we aim for Resolution

Jared's book outlines a system for resolving our emotional flaws.
It isn't an easy path, but it's a framework that I'm finding helpful which is why I'm writing about it here today.

### Map your Pattern

Here we aim to identify at high level our overall emotional volatility.
We do this by taking a detailed look out our *obvious mistakes* to see what our emotional reactions look like, so that we can learn to spot them in real time and minimize the damage.

### Identify the Roots of your Problem

To get to the real reason behind our problems, we need to uncover the hidden biases, illusions and flaws in our beliefs that can prevent us from progression our cause unnecessary ups and downs in our performance.

### Correct your Problem

Once we can understand our problem, we can tackle it head on by consistently applying the correction.
